How We Work#

How Heartbeat is planned and delivered: what everyone agrees on, what the team (people and agents) decides alone, how scope and reversals are handled, and what the board scorecard watches. Branching, commits, checks and releases are in CONTRIBUTING.md; delivery order and status are in TODO.md.

Outcomes over features#

Each roadmap phase is a user outcome: who can now do what, and the evidence that shows it. The outcomes are in the roadmap. A phase is done when its outcome is shown, not when its checklist is ticked.

  • Every checklist item serves the current phase's outcome, or a later phase's. If it serves neither, it does not go in.
  • Each phase ends with a demo of the outcome on real-shaped data (test data): the user, the question they ask, and what Heartbeat answers. The demo is the phase's exit check, ahead of the release.
  • Build a component in the same slice as its first caller. A part with no user and no caller waits.
  • When two items compete, the one that moves the current outcome wins over hardening, tooling or docs for a later phase.

Alignment and autonomy#

Scope grows because needs are found while building. That is expected; the answer is faster delivery, not a frozen plan. Delivery is fast when the team can act alone inside clear limits, and the owner answers what only the owner can answer, quickly.

The owner sets (alignment):

  • the phase outcomes and their order (roadmap)
  • the guardrails: architecture decisions, CONTRIBUTING.md and the agents' delivery policy (local to the owner's machine, not in git: what agents may commit, push and open without asking)
  • what needs the owner: merging, releases, deploys, spending, credentials and access, compliance, and any change to an outcome or a guardrail

The team decides alone (autonomy), inside those limits:

  • how to slice, build and test a phase's outcome
  • adding, splitting or reordering items that serve the current outcome
  • moving an item to a later phase, or to section 17, when it does not
  • recording a decision as an ADR when it follows from the guardrails

Owner actions are marked and answered fast. An item only the owner can do starts with Owner in TODO.md, with the date it became needed, or what it waits for until then. The aim is an answer within two working days. Status updates list open owner actions first, oldest first. Everything else continues around them.

Decisions come before the work that depends on them. A platform, compliance or organization question is answered, or explicitly deferred with the cost of waiting written down, before code builds on one answer.

Scope#

New items are welcome; they are sorted when they arrive, not when they are built:

A new need… Goes to
blocks the current phase's outcome, or is a defect the current phase
serves a later phase's outcome that phase's section
serves no planned outcome section 17, until an outcome needs it

Review findings follow the same rule. The scorecard shows items added against items finished each week; the aim is to finish more than is added over a month.

Test data#

Two kinds of data, for different questions:

PatientZero (ADR 0010) Replica of a real logging database
Answers Does Heartbeat produce the right signal when this fault happens? Is what Heartbeat shows useful on a real application's data?
Data Synthetic, shaped by the replica's survey; the answer is known Real: one week of a real installation's screens, integrations, slow SQL and users
Live Yes: load, faults, rotation, restarts No: the one surveyed was a stale extract (oslog_all) with the native log tables empty, deleted after the survey
Use for Fault scenarios, regressions, nightly CI Today: its schema and traffic profile only (ADR 0010). Outcome demos on its rows need a decision first

A replica holds personal data (ADR 0012): it is used only on the owner's machine and deleted once its numbers are taken, and only DDL and aggregates reach git.

Reversals#

A reversal is merged work or an accepted decision that is later undone or replaced: a component removed, a delivery path retired, an ADR superseded, a configuration contract broken. Plans reordered before anything is built are not reversals, and neither is ordinary fixing.

Reversals are part of learning; the aim is to find them sooner and cheaper. Each one is logged here when its pull request merges, with its cause:

  • Late information: a fact that existed but was not asked for in time
  • Built ahead of need: no user or caller when it was built
  • Wrong assumption: tested and found wrong
  • Changed need: the need itself changed
